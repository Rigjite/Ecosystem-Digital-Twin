## §21 — Cryosphere and mountain hazards

## Worked example: what a twin for the Langtang Lirung catchment would have shown

Had this panel existed for the catchment above Rasuwa and the Gyirong
corridor, on 25 August 2026 it would have shown: slope displacement up
to 40 cm over the previous 48 days (7 July–24 August; NISAR showed
~30 cm over 24 days) — a red watch level by displacement magnitude and
rate against the slope's own baseline (the threshold itself would have
had to be set from the slope's own baseline in advance; the draft does
not pretend the number would have been known to be "the" number). The
draft does not claim the rate was accelerating in the final days: the
ICIMOD synthesis does not establish that, and the source glacier itself
was moving at ~29 m/yr during 2014–2024 — the fifth-fastest of 148 ice
bodies in the area — with no acceleration trend, a pattern the report
reads as bedrock failure in which the ice played a passive role rather
than a glacier collapse. It would also have shown the gap that mattered
most: no live slope watch existed on that slope — the deformation was
identified retrospectively, and the instruments installed in the
corridor were river-level sensors, not slope sensors — recorded as a
data gap, which is itself a warning. The lake panel would have been
quiet, correctly, because this was not a GLOF. Downstream, the river
level and turbidity panel would have been the evacuation warning — but
with effectively no lead time near the source (the collapse struck at
08:37 NST and the surge reached the border corridor in ~7 minutes; the
Rasuwagadhi gauge stopped transmitting at 08:50; SMS alerts went out at
09:15–09:16, after the corridor was already hit) and hours of lead time
far downstream (the flood wave reached Galchhi at 10:28 and Devghat at
15:20; 679,295 SMS warnings were sent). The two temporary lakes the
surge left downstream breached on 28 and 31 August without secondary
disaster, so a barrier-lake watch on this event would have recorded the
lakes and their harmless breaches, not the flood itself. And the
pressures section (§17) would have carried the ignored warnings: civil
society warned the IFC in 2017 about earthquake, GLOF and climate risks
at the Upper Trishuli-1 hydropower project; the IFC's 2018 review
concluded GLOF risks were low; the Asian Development Bank's Independent
Panel of Experts found the September 2024 floods had put workers at
risk and in May 2025 recommended a climate resilience assessment that
had not been conducted — and at least 576 Upper Trishuli-1 workers were
among the missing. A twin's value here is partly in recording the gaps
and the ignored warnings, not only in the numbers.

## Second worked example for §21: what a twin for the Bozkurt–Ezine catchment would have shown

Rainfall began around noon on 10 August 2021 and continued until midnight, saturating the soils of the Bozkurt–Ezine watershed in Kastamonu Province, on Türkiye's western Black Sea coast. On 11 August, extremely intense rainfall in the upper watershed — 149.5 mm in three hours, peaking at 133.7 mm/h, exceeding a 500-year return period — sent a flash flood down the Ezine toward the town of Bozkurt (Kastamonu University NbS4LD case study). Bozkurt received 453 kg of rain per square metre, the highest in the event; 82 people died and 364 received medical support, and 2,483 people were rescued from the flood waters (AFAD 2022b, via Natural Hazards 2025). About 2,250 people were evacuated across the region (AFAD, via AP).

Had this panel existed for the Ezine catchment, on 10 August it would have shown rainfall intensity crossing red against the 500-year return-period anchor, and the river-level panel as the evacuation warning — with short lead time, hours rather than days. And it would have carried, before the rain, the standing vulnerability that the rain alone does not explain: construction had narrowed the Ezine's channel through Bozkurt from about 400 m to about 15 m, with residential buildings along the waterfront (geologists, via AP) — recorded in the pressures section (§17) as a known constraint, not discovered after the disaster. A twin's value here is partly in recording that the channel was already a constraint before the event, not only in the rainfall numbers. The early-warning communication gap is the subject of the Natural Hazards study itself: warnings reached people, but what people did with them varied — which is what the public status line (§20 watch levels and the plain-words status template) exists to fix: one status, one recommendation, in ordinary words.

Honest limits: the casualty, rescue and rainfall-total figures are verified against the peer-reviewed Natural Hazards paper, which cites AFAD 2022b; the intensity and return-period figures come from the Kastamonu University NbS4LD case study, which is a project analysis, not a peer-reviewed paper; the channel narrowing comes from AP reporting of geologists. No verified lead-time figure for the Bozkurt evacuation warning is recorded here — that cell is a gap, not a zero.

## §22 — Steppe and natural grassland

## Worked example: what a twin for the Daurian steppe would show

The Daurian steppe is the transboundary UNESCO World Heritage property
"Landscapes of Dauria" (inscribed 2017; the serial property covers 912,624
ha across Russia and Mongolia, in a Daurian steppe ecosystem of over
1 million km²), presented here from the Russian side: the Daursky State Nature Biosphere Reserve component (established 1987,
222,965 ha in southern Zabaykalsky Krai, on the Russian–Mongolian border),
which protects dry steppes and wetlands of South Siberia. The
property's Outstanding Universal Value cites its characteristic wildlife —
globally threatened birds including white-naped crane, hooded crane, swan
goose, relict gull, great bustard and saker falcon, and the endangered
tarbagan marmot — and its role as breeding and resting habitat on the
East Asian–Australasian Flyway, with up to 3 million birds in spring and
6 million in autumn, and as critical summer grounds and migration routes
of the Mongolian gazelle. The Dauria International Protected Area
agreement (DIPA, since 1994) runs joint scientific expeditions and a
joint inventory of animals and plants across the trilateral area.

Had this panel existed for the Daurian steppe, it would carry: steppe
vegetation composition and PFT shares from fixed plots (the *Stipa*
signal); bare ground and litter share as the erosion early warning;
forage biomass against its multi-year baseline; SOC in the steppe soils
(0–30 cm) under the FAO grassland baseline; erosion features (wind scour
is the Daurian risk, on open dry steppe); virgin-steppe area against the
plough line outside the reserve; shrub encroachment and fragmentation
from repeat remote sensing; burned area and fire return interval from
MODIS and reserve records; great bustard and saker falcon occupancy and
breeding success; tarbagan marmot colony density; Mongolian gazelle
seasonal presence; and the SDG 15.3.1 degraded-land composite for the
reserve and its buffer zone. The panel now carries published values for two of its four gaps: the June 2020 total count found 18,898 Mongolian gazelles in 475 groups across the Daursky + Dzeren Valley protected areas (density 3.30/km²; estimated abundance 21,701, 95% CI 20,616–22,785), with 30,050 gazelles across 10,089 km² at five Russian sites (Buuveibaatar et al. 2024, Oryx 58(6):759–768, DOI 10.1017/S0030605323001515); and tarbagan families counted 2010–2018 — 3,300–4,100 families (9,200–11,500 adults) in south-east Transbaikalia, including 75–85 families in the Daursky buffer zone north-east of Lake Zun-Torey and ~30 families at Lake Barun-Torey in the reserve itself, mean family size 2.8 ± 0.3 (Bazhenov 2019, Nat. Conserv. Res. 4(1):83–92, DOI 10.24189/ncr.2019.008). Two cells remain [NEXT DATA] gaps — burned area and SOC values — and a Daursky-reserve-only gazelle total and a current marmot density (individuals/ha) are likewise not published; those are gaps to fill from the reserve's own monitoring and the DIPA joint expeditions, not numbers to invent. Published context, not a Daursky value: tarbagan densities across the species' range are 1–7 ind/ha (IUCN Red List species account, Zenodo record 6819035), and a named-object comparator exists in Mongolia — Hustai National Park, ca. 1.27 ind/ha (13,213 marmots on 10,380 ha, Todgerel 2020, as cited in the marmot-decline literature — [provisional], a secondary citation, not a Daursky value; Hustai also carries 70.6 families/km², Kolesnikov et al. 2009). A twin that shows the panel with its gaps named is more useful than one that fills them with plausible figures.

## §23 — Livestock farm as an ecosystem

## Worked example: what a twin for the North Wyke Farm Platform would show

The North Wyke Farm Platform (NWFP) is an intensively instrumented
farm-scale ruminant research facility in Devon, south-west England, run
by Rothamsted Research: three instrumented farms over 63 hectares of
grazed grassland, with hydrological flows separated field by field so
that water and nutrient losses can be attributed to individual fields.
It is the UK's National Capability for grazing-systems research, and a
DEFRA Sustainable Intensification Platform research farm.

Had this panel existed for the NWFP, it would carry: pasture condition
and forage yield per field; stocking rate against carrying capacity (the
published sediment study recorded sheep at 0.15–1.00 UK livestock units
per hectare and cattle at 0–0.77 LU/ha); soil organic carbon per field
(the platform's data-mining study found higher SOC associated with
better animal performance and less nutrient loss into watercourses, and
higher stocking density with greater botanical diversity and elevated
SOC); soil compaction and infiltration under grazing; the farm nutrient
balance and manure application records; nitrate in field drains; methane
and ammonia fluxes; and slurry-store capacity and freeboard. The
panel's value here is partly in what it would refuse to say, and partly
in what the platform actually found: under best-practice grazing
management — appropriate stocking rates, grazing season matched to
rainfall and soil moisture, cattle housed indoors in the wet winter —
there was no detectable impact of livestock presence on sediment loss at
any of three timescales, and no indication of a field-scale effect on
soil bulk density; scheduled ploughing and reseeding, not grazing, was
the dominant sediment risk. The panel now carries published values for its three gaps, each with its scope stated: the nitrogen balance for 2011–2016, modelled from farm records (Carswell et al. 2019, Agric. Ecosyst. Environ. 283:106572, DOI 10.1016/j.agee.2019.106572) — only high-sugar grass positive at +11.9 kg N ha⁻¹ a⁻¹, high-sugar grass + clover transition lowest at −248 kg N ha⁻¹ a⁻¹, with leaching 36–92 and denitrification 61–76 kg N ha⁻¹ a⁻¹ (modelled, not measured); housed-cattle enteric methane 170–174 g/day, with no difference between silage diets (Meo-Filho et al. 2023, Animal 17:100726, DOI 10.1016/j.animal.2023.100726); and ammonia 2.5 g m⁻² d⁻¹ NH₃-N measured on experimental out-wintering pads (Dumont et al. 2012, Agric. Ecosyst. Environ. 160:82–90, DOI 10.1016/j.agee.2012.04.019), with a modelled farmlet range of 4.1–9.0 kg N ha⁻¹ a⁻¹ (Carswell et al. 2019). Still open [NEXT DATA] gaps: a farm-gate phosphorus balance, a whole-platform annual methane total, and a directly measured whole-farmlet ammonia flux — gaps to fill from the platform's own records, not numbers to invent. A twin that shows the panel with its gaps named is more useful than one that fills them with plausible figures.

## §24 — Peatlands and bogs

## Worked example: the Moscow region peatlands — fire, then rewetting

The worked example is the "Restoring Peatlands in Russia" project, run
by Wetlands International together with the Institute of Forest Science
of the Russian Academy of Sciences and the Michael Succow Foundation,
within German–Russian cooperation, after the summer 2010 fires. The
facts, as published: Landsat-5 TM mapping put the area burnt in the
peatlands of the Moscow region in 2010 at 19.8 thousand hectares, most of
it in the eastern and northern sectors of the region, and burning peat
caused 90% of the smoke that choked Moscow (GFMC, 2011); the Russian
government set a three-year programme to flood 65,000 hectares of
fire-risk peat bogs (77,000 ha in the full schedule to the end of 2013),
and the Moscow Province Administration rewetted 73,000 hectares of
fire-prone peatlands in 2010–2013; the project mapped the Moscow Province
peatlands, built a GIS-based decision-support system for prioritising
restoration sites, and set a 35,000-hectare target for restoration of
natural peatland vegetation within the roughly 75,000 hectares rewetted
(Wetlands International / PBL reporting). Had this section's panel existed for a Moscow-region peat
site in spring 2010, it would have shown: a water-table depth far below
the surface across the drained area, a high ditch density, no Sphagnum
cover on the milled extraction fields, and a peat-fire watch level already
red on the water-table indicator before any fire started — the three
numbers that, together, are the fire warning. After rewetting, the same
panel would show the recovery the project reports: ditch blocks
installed, water table raised toward the surface, and the fire
occurrence indicator quiet. The panel would also have recorded the
honest limit: rewetting is not restoration. A rewetted extraction field
with a raised water table and no Sphagnum is recorded as "rewetted, peat
formation not yet resumed" — the vegetation indicator, not the water
table, carries that judgement, and it may take years to change.

## §25 — Marine and coastal

## Worked example: what a twin for the Great Barrier Reef would have shown

The Great Barrier Reef is the best-documented case for this section,
because the Australian Institute of Marine Science (AIMS) has run a
Long-Term Monitoring Program (LTMP) on it since 1986, and its
2024/2025 condition report — published 6 August 2025, from surveys of
124 reefs between August 2024 and May 2025 — records the 2024 mass
bleaching event in the terms this section uses. Had this panel existed
for the Reef, in early 2024 it would have shown: sea-surface temperature
anomalies and a marine heatwave through February–March 2024; DHW
accumulating past the 4 °C-weeks bleaching level and, in places, past
8 °C-weeks, the mass-bleaching-and-mortality level; and then the field
confirmation — hard coral cover falling from 39.8% to 30.0% in the north
(a 24.8% relative decline), from 33.2% to 28.6% in the centre (13.9%),
and from 38.9% to 26.9% in the south (30.6%), the southern region falling
below its historical average of 29.3%. Across the 124 reefs, 48% lost
coral cover, 42% showed no net change and 10% gained; individual reefs
lost up to 70.8%. AIMS identified bleaching as the main cause of
mortality, alongside cyclones, freshwater flooding and crown-of-thorns
starfish, and fast-growing Acropora corals — the ones that had driven the
recovery between 2017 and 2024 — were among the most severely affected.
The twin's value here is in holding the two panels apart and together:
the DHW panel would have warned *before* the survey, the cover panel
records *what actually happened*, the recruitment panel would now be the
watch that matters (whether the reef replaces what it lost), and the
crown-of-thorns panel would carry the compounding pressure that bleaching
alone does not explain. The 2024 event was the fifth mass bleaching on
the Reef since 2016 and part of the fourth global event; the LTMP record
is what makes the "object baseline" in this section a real thing rather
than a phrase — 39 years of surveys against which a single summer's loss
can be judged.

## §26 — Urban ecosystems — the city as an ecosystem

## Worked example: what a twin for New York City would have shown

Take the city as the object, with the boundary the five boroughs and
the reference the rural ring around them. On the heat panel, the twin
would have shown an Urban Heat Index of 9.7 °F for the average
resident (Climate Central 2024) — the largest of the 65 US cities in
that study — with the district panel doing the real work: some parts
of the city as high as about 13 °F, about 7.5 °F in Central Park against about 12 °F on
the adjoining Upper East Side, and Staten Island cooler overall than
Manhattan. The district rule would have put the watch level on the
hottest districts, not the 9.7 °F average, and the heat-health panel
would have carried the nights that do not cool. On the water panel,
the twin would have shown a system about 60% combined, with 398
combined-sewer overflow outfalls discharging approximately 18 billion
gallons of combined sewage a year (NYSDEC) — overflows down 80%
since the mid-1980s (NYC DEP, cited in the NYS Comptroller's 2019
report on combined sewers), which the twin would report as a trend,
not as a clean bill of health, because 18 billion gallons a year is
still the state. The
green-infrastructure panel would have carried the programme target
alongside the measured outcome: the NYC Green Infrastructure
Program's commitment of about 1.67 billion gallons of CSO flow
removed annually by 2030 (2023 CSO Order modification; the 2012
agreement estimate was about 1.5 billion gallons), recorded as a
target, with the measured retention reported
against it year by year. The canopy panel would have shown where the
shade is and where it is not — the Comptroller's living-infrastructure
review records double-digit temperature differences on hot days
between neighbourhoods with abundant canopy and those with little —
and the twin's value would be in holding the three panels together:
the hottest districts, the least canopy, and the fastest runoff are
the same districts, and a planting programme that does not know that
is planting in the wrong place. What the twin would *not* have shown
is a single number for "how green" or "how hot" New York is. There
isn't one.

## §27 — Aquaculture — fish farming

## Worked example: what a twin for Norwegian Atlantic salmon cage farming would have shown

No single named farm is used here, because farm-level production data are
commercial and not published per farm; the worked example is therefore the
Norwegian salmon sector as a production area, which is the scale at which
the public data exist. Had this panel existed for Norwegian salmon farming
in 2024–2025, it would have shown, on one page, a system under strain on
several indicators at once. Mortality: 60 million farmed salmon recorded
dead or discarded in 2024, down from 65 million in 2023 (Norwegian
Institute of Marine Research, Risk Report 2025, via Salmon Business) —
still elevated, with six production zones at high risk to fish welfare.
Disease: the Norwegian Veterinary Institute's Fish Health Report 2025
records a record 3,918 sea-lice treatment weeks in 2025 (about 700 more
than the previous year), 99 cases of amoebic gill disease (a marked
increase) and 40 cases of infectious pancreatic necrosis (roughly three
times the previous year), with handling injuries from delousing ranked the
leading cause of mortality and reduced welfare in harvest-size salmon.
Feed: regional FCR in 2011–2020 ran 1.06–1.17 kg dry feed per kg fish
(Aquacult. Environ. Interact. 2023) — efficient by any livestock standard,
and therefore a poor early warning on its own: a farm can convert feed
well while its fish are dying of lice treatments. Escapes: a 27,000-fish
escape in production area PO10 and a 15,000-fish escape in PO5 in 2024–
early 2025, with the share of escaped farmed salmon in rivers still
declining overall. Genetic-introgression risk was moderate-to-high in 21
of 34 investigated wild populations (Taranger et al. 2015, on 2010–2012
data). And the wild-fish panel: sea-lice pressure raised the risk
classification from low to moderate in
production areas PO8, PO9, PO11 and PO12; separately, the
genetic-introgression risk classification was raised to high in PO9 in
part because of monitoring gaps, and lowered in PO4, PO11 and PO12 — a
data gap recorded as a risk, not as a clean bill of health. A twin's value here is in holding the panels
together: the mortality panel, the treatment panel and the wild-fish
panel describe one system, and a farm that reports a good eFCR while its
mortality and treatment counts climb is not a healthy farm.

## §28 — Desert and arid lands

## Worked example: what a twin for the Ternata Oasis would have shown

The Ternata Oasis in southeastern Morocco is a real, published case
(Moumane et al., *Scientific Reports* (2026) 16:9212, doi:10.1038/
s41598-026-41216-9 — multidecadal monitoring framework, 1984–2024, full Landsat archive in Google Earth Engine, Gradient Tree
Boosting classifier at 87.2% overall accuracy, outperforming Random Forest
at 85.0% and CART at 82.0%). Had this panel existed for the oasis, it would
have shown the story as a set of separate panels that agree: the oasis
extent and grove-condition panel contracting, with healthy oasis area
shrinking while desertified land expanded; the oasis-water panel showing
groundwater levels dropping markedly and water salinity exceeding the
critical thresholds for date-palm survival; and the pressure panel (§17)
carrying what the farmers themselves reported in interview — increasing
salinity stress, prohibitively high well-deepening costs, youth
outmigration, and more palm-grove fires, largely from dead and dying trees
made more flammable by drought and salt. The vegetation panel alone would
have been ambiguous — a dry decade browns any oasis — but the vegetation
breach standing *unexplained* against rainfall, next to a falling
water-table trend and a rising EC trend, is the desertification signal this
section is built to produce. And the twin's value here is partly in
recording what the numbers cannot: that the failure mode is water plus
salt together, and that the response — well-deepening — is itself priced
out of reach, so the trigger–action–response plan must offer something
other than "drill deeper". The draft does not pretend to know the oasis's
own thresholds — the study as reported does not publish a single
contraction percentage or a single groundwater depth in the sources
consulted — and it does not invent them.

## §29 — Tundra and polar

## Worked example: what a twin for the Toolik Lake tundra would show

Toolik Lake is a large oligotrophic kettle lake at 68°38′N, 149°43′W,
elevation 720 m, in the northern foothills of the Brooks Range, Alaska.
Long-term aquatic research there began in 1975 and terrestrial work in
1976; the site joined the US Long Term Ecological Research (LTER)
network in 1987 and is operated by the University of Alaska Fairbanks
Institute of Arctic Biology, with the field station celebrating its
50th anniversary in 2025. The surrounding tundra includes all the
common arctic tundra types, with continuous permafrost to a depth of
~200 m, continuous daylight from mid-May to late July, and a snow-free
season normally from late May to late September. The Arctic LTER
programme's stated aim is a predictive understanding of the arctic
landscape near Toolik Lake — tundra, streams and lakes and their
interactions — and its long-term measurements include tundra flowering,
air temperature, solar radiation and biomass, alongside lake and stream
chemistry and biology. Forty kilometres north-northwest of Toolik lies
the Anaktuvuk River burn site (~1,000 km², the 2007 fire), studied as
part of the same programme.

Had this panel existed for the Toolik Lake tundra, it would carry:
tundra vegetation composition and PFT shares from the long-running
field plots (the shrub-expansion signal the station tracks); shrub
cover and height against the plot baseline; NDVI/MaxNDVI against the
multi-year satellite baseline, with the field plots as the check on
what the greenness means; active-layer thickness from CALM-style
probing and borehole temperature per GTN-P (the ground-temperature row
itself held in §21); thermokarst features from repeat remote sensing of
the watershed, including the thaw disturbances within helicopter range
of the station; SOC in the tundra soils (0–30 cm) under the NCSCD
baseline; snow cover duration and rain-on-snow events from the station
meteorology; thaw-pond and lake area in the watershed; caribou
seasonal presence (a herd was photographed traversing the tundra near
the station in August 2024); the lemming/vole cycle state from small-
mammal trapping; migratory bird breeding success and insect emergence
timing from the station's phenology records; the Anaktuvuk burn scar as
the fire panel, with post-fire vegetation and permafrost recovery
tracked; and CO₂/CH₄ fluxes from the station's flux measurements where
they exist. The panel now carries published values for five of its six gaps: deciduous shrub cover rose from 13.6% in 1990 to 31.7% in 2023 (+132.3%) on the Toolik 1 km² grid (Betway-May et al. 2025, Glob. Change Biol. 31:e70155, DOI 10.1111/gcb.70155); mean maximum active-layer thickness is 28–52 cm with no significant trend, while permafrost temperature at 20 m depth rose 0.8 °C (Hobbie et al. 2017, Ambio 46(Suppl. 1):160–173, DOI 10.1007/s13280-016-0870-x); the Central Arctic Herd numbered 26,600 in the 5 July 2025 photocensus, down from 34,600 in 2022 (ADF&G release, 7 January 2026); SOC stock in the flux footprint is 5–25 kg C m⁻² (Howard et al. 2020, Biogeosciences 17:4025–4042, DOI 10.5194/bg-17-4025-2020); and net CO₂/CH₄ fluxes were CH₄ 4.9 and CO₂ 485 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹ in year 1, CH₄ 3.7 and CO₂ 89 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹ in year 2 (Howard et al. 2020). One cell remains a [NEXT DATA] gap — lemming densities at Toolik, described in the literature only qualitatively as 'low numbers'; the NEON small-mammal dataset (since 2019) is the identified next source — a gap to fill from the Arctic LTER data portal and the station's own records, not numbers to invent. A twin that shows the panel with its gaps named is more useful than one that fills them with plausible figures.


## Registered virtual sensors — worked example: Beira Lake and the Langtang slope

The first registrations in the skill: six virtual sensors, filled in the
Object Passport form (`assets/templates.md` §1, "Virtual sensors" block),
for two objects whose published records already contain the calibration
stations. Nothing here is a live twin — it is the registration a twin
would carry, with every formula and every skill score taken from a
published source, and every gap named instead of filled. A registered
virtual sensor is QC'd and alerted exactly like a physical sensor, and
when it leaves calibration its readings go to the [НЕТ ДАННЫХ] register —
never silently used. One of the six carries a Beira-local published skill
score (the Secchi-depth sensor); the other five carry the published
generic validation of their algorithm and a named [NEXT DATA] gap for the
local score. Verified 2026-10-28 (T91): those five gaps are verified
negative results — the published record contains no data from which a
local score could be computed — not merely comparisons nobody has run.

### The objects

Beira Lake, Colombo, Sri Lanka — four interconnected basins (East Lake,
Galle Face Lake, West Lake, Southwest/Gangarama Lake), present extent
about 65.4 ha, reduced from an estimated original 162 ha by reclamation
(Prasad et al. 2024, Water 16(11), 1616, DOI 10.3390/w16111616). The
paper's in-situ record is 80 surface samples — 29 in the dry season
(September 2022) and 51 in the wet season (March 2023) — at 0–10 cm
depth, 08:00–12:00, triplicate per station: dissolved oxygen, electrical
conductivity, surface water temperature, pH, total dissolved solids and
Secchi disk depth measured in situ; COD, ammonia, total nitrogen, total
phosphorus, heavy metals (Cd, Cr, Pb, Hg) and anions in the laboratory.
Published state: WPI 2.53 (dry) / 2.38 (wet); TSI(TP) 129.09 (wet);
HPI 133.86 (wet only); maturity 2, one desk case, zero live deployments.
The Langtang Lirung detachment slope, Nepal–China border — the slope
whose rock-ice mass detached on 26 August 2026 (ICIMOD 2026, see the §21
source list). No ground instrument watched that slope; the deformation
was found retrospectively in Sentinel-1 data.

### Sensor 1 — beira-chla-s2-ndci: chlorophyll-a from Sentinel-2

- Physical quantity: chlorophyll-a concentration, µg/L.
- Proxy source + deterministic formula: Sentinel-2 MSI L2A, bands B04
  (665 nm) and B05 (705 nm). NDCI = (R705 − R665) / (R705 + R665);
  Chl-a = 194.325·NDCI² + 86.115·NDCI + 14.039 (Mishra & Mishra 2012,
  Remote Sens. Environ. 117:394–406). The coefficients are the MERIS

- Calibration station: the Prasad et al. 2024 in-situ chlorophyll-a by
  chemical analysis at the Beira sampling stations — published only as

### Sensor 3 — beira-extent-s2-mndwi: water surface extent from Sentinel-2

- Physical quantity: water surface area, ha (and the water mask).
- Proxy source + deterministic formula: Modified Normalised Difference
  Water Index (Xu 2006, Int. J. Remote Sens. 27:3025–3033):
  MNDWI = (Green − SWIR) / (Green + SWIR), Sentinel-2 B03 (560 nm) and
  B11 (1610 nm); water where MNDWI > 0, checked against the surveyed
  shoreline. MNDWI is chosen over the McFeeters NDWI (1996) because it
  suppresses built-up land noise — Beira sits in central Colombo.
- Calibration station: the surveyed Beira extent, about 65.4 ha
  (Prasad et al. 2024, §2.1); sampling-station coordinates in the
  paper's Table S1.
- Skill score (SS_RMSE): published validation of the reference product
  — JRC Global Surface Water (Pekel et al. 2016, Nature 540:418–422,
  DOI 10.1038/nature20584): under 1% false water detections and under
  5% of water surfaces missed, from 40,000+ control points over 32
  years; permanent-water accuracy 99.5–99.7% by Landsat sensor. That
  validation is for the Landsat-based JRC product, not for Sentinel-2
  MNDWI at Beira. Beira-local skill score for the Sentinel-2 mask:
  [NEXT DATA] — verified 2026-10-28 (T91): not computable from the
  published record. The surveyed extent, about 65.4 ha (Prasad et al.
  2024, §2.1), is a single total, not a shoreline polygon or a mask —
  a mask accuracy (false/missed water) cannot be derived from one
  total, and no published MNDWI mask for Beira exists. An
  area-difference check (MNDWI mask area vs 65.4 ha) is a weak proxy
  only — seasonal water level and shoreline definition differ — and is
  recorded here as a defined future computation, not performed and not
  a mask score. What is needed: a surveyed shoreline polygon for a
  dated scene, or the authors' station coordinates (Table S1) plus a
  same-day clear Sentinel-2 scene, to score the mask point by point.
- Validity window: 10–20 m, ~5-day revisit; cloud-free scenes only.
  The mask is a classification (water / not water), so its score is an
  accuracy, not an RMSE — reported as such, never converted into a
  pseudo-RMSE.
- QC and alert: as a physical sensor; a shrinking extent is a slow
  signal, watched on the multi-year baseline, not alerted per scene.

### Sensor 4 — beira-turbidity-s2-dogliotti: turbidity from Sentinel-2

- Physical quantity: turbidity, FNU (ISO 7027, 860 nm side-scatter
  against Formazin).
- Proxy source + deterministic formula: Dogliotti et al. 2015 (Remote
  Sens. Environ. 156:157–168), Eq. (1):
  T = A^T · ρw(λ) / (1 − ρw(λ) / C^T); 645 nm: A^T = 228.1 FNU,
  C^T = 0.1641; 859 nm: A^T = 3078.9 FNU, C^T = 0.2112 (coefficients
  from Nechad et al. 2010 / Dogliotti et al. 2011, Table 2 of the
  paper). Use 645 nm when ρw(645) < 0.05, 859 nm when ρw(645) > 0.07,
  linear blend between (T = (1 − w)·T645 + w·T859). Sentinel-2 nearest
  bands are B04 (665 nm) and B8A (865 nm); the published coefficients
  are for MODIS 645/859 nm, so the Sentinel-2 application is an
  adaptation (the ACOLITE processor implements the Nechad/Dogliotti
  algorithms for Sentinel-2 bands) — registered with that caveat.
- Calibration station: none at Beira — Prasad et al. 2024 measured no
  in-situ turbidity. The related in-situ measure is Secchi disk depth;
  the QAA Secchi-depth chain (Sensor 5) is the transparency
  cross-check, not a turbidity calibration.
- Skill score (SS_RMSE): published validation — Dogliotti et al. 2015,
  Table 4, all sites (n = 106): slope 1.07, intercept −1.29 FNU,
  r = 0.97, RMSE 20.5 FNU, mean relative error 13.7%, bias 4.8%,
  range 1.8–988 FNU. Beira-local SS_RMSE: [NEXT DATA] — verified
  2026-10-28 (T91): structurally impossible from the published record
  — no in-situ turbidity exists in the published Beira record, so there
  is nothing to score the satellite retrieval against. The only
  indirect check is the Secchi-depth transparency chain (Sensor 5),
  which is not a turbidity calibration. What is needed: a field
  turbidity campaign (ISO 7027 instrument) paired with same-day
  Sentinel-2 scenes.
- Validity window: published to 1000 FNU. FNU is not interchangeable
  with NTU without stating the instrument — the unit travels with the
  number.
- QC and alert: as a physical sensor; alert only on a sustained breach.

### Sensor 5 — beira-zsd-s2-qaa: Secchi disk depth from Sentinel-2

- Physical quantity: Secchi disk depth (water transparency), m.
- Proxy source + deterministic formula: Lee et al. 2015 mechanistic
  model (Remote Sens. Environ. 169:139–149):
  Z_SD = [1 / (2.5 · Min(Kd(λi)))] · ln(|0.14 − Rrs,m| / 0.013), where
  Min(Kd) is the minimum diffuse attenuation coefficient within
  410–665 nm and Rrs,m is the remote-sensing reflectance at that
  wavelength; Kd from the quasi-analytical algorithm (QAA, Lee et al.
  2002/2009). Prasad et al. 2024 applied it on Sentinel-2 bands 443,
  490, 560 and 665 nm.
- Calibration station: the Prasad et al. 2024 in-situ Secchi disk
  depth (standard 20 cm disk) at the Beira stations.
- Skill score (SS_RMSE): Beira-local published validation — Prasad et
  al. 2024, §3.3.3: n = 43 (March 2023), R² = 0.670, RMSE 0.049 m, mean
  absolute deviation 0.042 m, mean absolute percentage deviation 26.1%.
  The SS_RMSE for this sensor is 0.049 m. The global validation of the
  model (Lee et al. 2015: N = 338, R² = 0.96, ~18% mean absolute
  error) covers 1–30+ m visibility; Beira ZSD (maximum 0.26 m in 2016,
  0.45 m in 2023; below 1 m in West and Gangarama lakes) sits far below
  that range, so the Beira-local RMSE governs, not the global one.
- Validity window: cloud-free scenes only; 10–20 m, ~5-day revisit.
- QC and alert: as a physical sensor; transparency is a slow signal,
  watched against the 2016–2023 baseline (maximum ZSD 0.26 → 0.45 m).

### Sensor 6 — langtang-displacement-s1-insar: slope displacement from Sentinel-1

- Physical quantity: line-of-sight ground displacement, cm (and rate,
  mm/day).
- Proxy source + deterministic formula: Sentinel-1 C-band
  differential InSAR / SBAS time series: line-of-sight displacement
  Δd = λ·Δφ / (4π), λ = 5.55 cm (5.405 GHz). Displacement needs an
  InSAR pair — two passes, days apart (SNAP / ASF HyP3 processing;
  `scripts/fetch_satellite.py` searches Sentinel-1 scenes only and does
  not compute displacement). Observation: up to 40 cm line-of-sight
  deformation over 48 days (7 July–24 August 2026) on the Langtang
  detachment slope (ICIMOD 2026); NISAR showed ~30 cm over 24 days as

- Calibration station: none on the detachment slope — no GNSS station
  watched it; the deformation was identified retrospectively. There is
  no local ground truth to score against.
- Skill score (SS_RMSE): published accuracy anchors — Sentinel-1
  InSAR against second-order levelling and GNSS: mean deviations
  typically within ±10 mm, mean error deviation within ±5 mm
  (Beijing study, Remote Sens. 2026, 18, 1274); formal line-of-sight
  noise ~1.2 mm against an actual error of 13.8 mm vertical and
  17.2 mm east–west once atmosphere, orbit and unwrapping systematics
  are included (Dingri 2025 earthquake error budget, Remote Sens.
  2026, 18, 2252) — formal errors understate the real uncertainty.
  The 40 cm Langtang signal is roughly 20–30 times the error budget.
  Langtang-local SS_RMSE: [NEXT DATA] — verified 2026-10-28 (T91):
  no ground truth exists for that slope, and none can ever be collected
  retrospectively — the slope collapsed. The ICIMOD preliminary
  synthesis (13 October 2026) is satellite-only (Sentinel-1 InSAR,
  NISAR, seismic, drone survey of the aftermath, hydrology); the

  Sens., DOI 10.1007/s12524-026-02611-x, published 16 October 2026)

- Validity window: 6–12-day revisit; coherence is lost over snow,

- QC and alert: as a physical sensor; a sustained displacement-rate

## §30 — Forest

## Worked example: what a twin for Harvard Forest would show

Harvard Forest is a ~1,500 ha temperate mixed forest in Petersham,
Massachusetts (42.55° N, 72.23° W), established 1907, a US Long Term
Ecological Research (LTER) site since 1988. Hemlock (*Tsuga canadensis*)
covers about a quarter of the forest; the rest is hardwoods (red oak, red
maple, birch) and white pine. It is the best-measured temperate forest in
the world for carbon: a flux tower has run continuously since the early
1990s, and the Hemlock Removal Experiment (HF-HeRE, Ellison et al. 2010)
deliberately removed hemlock two ways — girdling (to mimic the woolly
adelgid) and logging — so the twin can see what each disturbance does.

Had this panel existed for Harvard Forest, it would carry: forest type
(temperate mixed forest; hemlock ~25%); pretreatment basal area
29.6–53.0 m²/ha across the eight HF-HeRE plots, hemlock 53–73% of basal
area in hemlock plots (Ellison et al. 2010); the carbon pool shares — live
biomass ~50% of ecosystem carbon, soil to 45 cm ~45%, woody debris ~5%
(Finzi et al. 2020); net uptake 2.0 ± 0.4 Mg C/ha/yr in 1993–2000
(biometry 1.6 ± 0.4; 60% of the storage in live biomass; Barford et al.
2001); NEP ~300 g C/m²/yr in hardwood stands and ~450 g C/m²/yr in
hemlock stands, NEP up 93% over 1992–2015 — and then the flip: the
hemlock flux tower turned into a net carbon source after 2014, when the
hemlock woolly adelgid (arrived ~2009) had killed the hemlock that was
expected to functionally disappear by ~2025 (Finzi et al. 2020); the
disturbance experiment itself — girdled hemlocks died within 30 months,
logged plots lost 60–70% of basal area, dead stumps and snags increased
2–3 orders of magnitude within two years of girdling, and regeneration
differs between girdled and logged plots (Ellison et al. 2010; Orwig et
al. 2013); and the understory — ungulate browsers forage differently in
adelgid-killed and logged stands, so the disturbance type changes the
top-down control of regeneration (Faison et al. 2016).

The lesson the twin carries: Harvard Forest is the proof that a forest's
carbon sink is not a permanent property — the same stand that took up
2.0 Mg C/ha/yr in the 1990s became a net source in its hemlock part after
one pest arrived. The twin's job is to catch that flip: the flux row
turning from sink to source is the red band, and it fires years before the
canopy visibly thins. The second lesson is that the disturbance type
decides the next forest: girdled stands keep their dead wood standing and
regenerate differently from logged stands where the boles are gone and the
soil is compacted. A twin that records only "forest lost" misses the
difference.

- Current canopy-cover percentage and current deadwood volume at Harvard
  Forest — no published current value was found in the sources checked.
- Current aboveground biomass stock in t C/ha — the published values are
  pool shares, not a current stock figure.
- Current regeneration density (seedlings/ha).
- Landscape fragmentation metrics for the surrounding landscape — no
  published value found.
- Forest fire regime and protected-area / certification status are not
  applicable here (no fire regime at this site; a research forest, not a
  certified production forest) — not gaps, recorded as not applicable.

## Second worked example for §30: what a twin for the Barguzinsky Reserve and the Buryatia taiga edge would show

The Barguzinsky State Nature Biosphere Reserve is the oldest nature
reserve in Russia, created 29 December 1916 as a sable reserve — 20–30
sables lived there at creation; today 1,500–2,000 (density 1–2 per km²).
It covers 374,322 ha, including a three-kilometre strip of Lake Baikal
waters (about 150,000 ha) and a biosphere polygon (111,114 ha), on the
north-eastern shore of Baikal; it is IUCN category Ia, a UNESCO biosphere
reserve since 1986 and part of the Lake Baikal World Heritage property
(No. 754) since 1996, managed by FGBU "Zapovednoe Podlemorye". Its
habitats are mountain larch and dark-coniferous taiga (spruce, fir,
Siberian pine), mountain tundra, bogs and subalpine meadows; 41 mammal
species live there, among them brown bear, sable, elk, musk deer, lynx
and wolverine. (The land area differs between sources — English Wikipedia
gives 2,482 km² — because water and land are counted differently; the
example carries the total 374,322 ha with its composition stated.)

Autumn 2026 in Buryatia is the sharpest bear season in years. For the
whole of 2025 the republic recorded 47 cases of bears coming out to
settlements; in the 2026 season the forced cull passed 149 bears by
14 September, 331 by 3 October and 436 by 20 October, with 15–20 reports
a day at the peak, 24 brigades and 81 hunters at work, and 12 most
problematic territories. Six attacks on people were recorded that autumn,
two people died — both in the forest; no attack was recorded inside a
settlement. Schools in Zakamensk went to distance learning. The most
problematic districts were Kabansky, Pribaikalsky, Barguzinsky,
Zakamensky, Dzhidinsky and Kurumkansky; exits were also recorded around
Ulan-Ude, in garden cooperatives, at dumps, tourist bases, roadsides and
even city cemeteries.

The numbers did not explode — the behaviour changed. State monitoring
counts 6,113 brown bears in Buryatia in 2026, against 6,276 in 2025,
5,385 in 2021 and 4,625 in 2011 — up about a third in 15 years, down 3%
in 2026; density 0.21 per 1,000 ha. The harvest limit is 1,272 animals
(406 public grounds, 866 leased), about 900 permits are issued and about
300 bears a year are actually harvested; the service calls an annual
removal of up to 1,800 "painless". For comparison, Russia as a whole
holds 307,900 brown bears (Ministry of Natural Resources, 2026) against
about 130,000 in 1990.

The cause the specialists name first is hunger: in 2026 the Siberian
taiga gave almost no cedar nut — the main fattening feed — and berries
and mushrooms failed too; in Buryatia the berry crop fell and cedar nut
was "in many districts simply absent" (Burprirodnadzor). The animals
that come out are almost all young males of 2–3 years, pushed out of
their territories by adults (Burprirodnadzor). The second row of causes
is food attractants at housing — unauthorised dumps, garden berries,
unattended livestock — and habituation: bears that have learned to feed
at dumps and roadsides no longer fear people. The background is a
population that grew while the harvest stayed far below the limit, and a
collapsed export market for bear products that took the economic sense
out of hunting.

The twin's job here is the chain feed → animal → event → warning. The
mast index (§30) is the earliest signal — it fails in August–September,
weeks before the exits; the conflict-report series (§17) is the watched
indicator; the watch levels (§20) for the taiga edge run: green is a
normal mast year with isolated reports, of the order of the 2025 baseline
(fewer than 1–2 a week across the republic); yellow is a mast failure
with reports rising to several a week per district and the first exits of
2–3-year-old animals — warn residents, close trails, remove attractants,
step up patrols; red is a repeat exit by the same animal, an attack on a
person or livestock, or a bear inside a settlement, near a school or a
tourist base — dispatch a brigade, regulate numbers, move schools to
distance learning, as in Zakamensk in September 2026. The
sustained-breach rule governs: one visit to a dump is an event; three
visits in a week in one village is the sustained signal.

Reliability note — read before using any figure: the Burprirodnadzor and
Ministry of Natural Resources figures reach us as relayed by the
republican and federal press in October 2026; the primary departmental
summaries are not published openly. Where two sources disagree (bear
numbers in the reserve; the reserve's land area; the national death
toll), the example carries both values with their sources. No figure in
this example is invented; gaps are named below.

## §31 — Cropland

## Worked example: what a twin for the GEPIS rice paddies in Sri Lanka would show

The worked example is the rice node of the linked Sri Lanka twin in the author's
article (Baldanova et al., v9.2): a conceptual simulation linking the heavily polluted
urban Beira Lake (Colombo), a subcritical-water sludge-processing node, and a rice
cultivation site under the joint Japanese–Sri Lankan GEPIS initiative. The rice site
is a starter area of about 40 acres (about 16 ha) at Mayurapura, Hambantota district,
growing premium Japonica rice on weathered soils structured with tanakura clay and
organic amendments, under regular agrochemical and toxicological control of soils and
produce — with a project target yield of 5.5–6.2 t/ha for sale in the premium segment
at 450–600 LKR/kg. Those yield and price figures are project targets from the article,
not observed yields: no measured yield, water use, methane flux or soil-carbon value
for the GEPIS site has been published, and the twin records them as targets until
measurements exist.

The lake node is measured, not conceptual: published monitoring data show Beira Lake
severely polluted year-round, Water Pollution Index 2.4–2.5 against a threshold of 1.0,
eutrophic to hypereutrophic (Prasad et al. 2024, Water 16(11), 1616, DOI
10.3390/w16111616). The linked twin evaluates sediment dredging, inflow interception,
green infrastructure and their combination against a no-action baseline — and permits
agricultural application of treated sediment strictly upon verified compliance with
safety standards. The cropland rows are the gate: the sediment is a resource only if
the field's soil-toxicology rows say so.

Had this panel existed for the GEPIS site, it would carry: cropland extent (about
16 ha starter area, with the expansion perspective recorded, not assumed); crop type
(Japonica rice, single-crop paddy system); the national yield context — 2022/23 Maha
3,731 kg/ha and 2023 Yala 4,128 kg/ha (Department of Census and Statistics, Paddy
Statistics, national level, revised series); paddy evapotranspiration Maha 470 mm, Yala 677 mm (Wanasinghe et al. 2023);
the water regime (continuous flooding as the baseline, AWD as the tested alternative —
single drainage scales CH₄ to 0.71, multiple drainage to 0.55 under the IPCC 2019
Refinement, with the N₂O trade-off counted alongside); the fertiliser row carrying the
national history — Sri Lanka banned chemical fertiliser imports in 2021, yields
collapsed across seasons, and the ban was reversed — and the recovery: the USDA puts
2025/26 production at a record 3.7 Mt (milled) (USDA ERS Rice Outlook RCS-26H,
15 September 2026), from a record 1.2 M ha at 4.62 t/ha (rough) as reported in the
trade press citing USDA — area and yield not yet read from a USDA document — driven
by fertiliser imports more than 60% above the previous year; and the field-margin row, which is where the
biodiversity of a paddy landscape actually lives.

The lesson the twin carries: the GEPIS target of 5.5–6.2 t/ha is about one and a half
times the national average (3.7–4.1 t/ha in the DCS revised series), and the twin's job is to show whether the water, nutrient and soil
numbers support it or whether it is bought with fertiliser and water the site cannot
sustain — the 2021 ban and its reversal are the national-scale proof that the yield
row and the fertiliser row cannot be read apart. The second lesson is that the
sediment-substitution question is decided by the field's soil rows, not by the lake's
need to dispose of sediment: a twin that lets the lake's problem set the field's
inputs has the arrow backwards.

## §32 — Non-peat wetlands and marshes

## Worked example: what a twin for the Everglades would show

The Everglades is a ~10,360 km² freshwater marsh in southern Florida
(25–27° N), a "river of grass" that flowed as sheet flow from Lake
Okeechobee to Florida Bay. Before regional drainage began in the 1880s
it covered about 4,000 square miles; about half remains today ("less than
half", Florida Museum; "just over half", Davis & Ogden 1994 overview), mostly
broken into water-conservation impoundments controlled by levees and
canals, with only Everglades National Park at the south end still
free-flowing (Davis & Ogden 1994; Florida Museum). It is the
best-measured freshwater marsh in the world for hydrology and nutrients:
the Comprehensive Everglades Restoration Plan (CERP) is the largest
ecosystem-restoration programme ever attempted, and the Florida Coastal
Everglades LTER site has run continuous monitoring for decades. It is
also the marsh where the twin's rows were written: hydroperiod,
phosphorus, cattail, birds.

Had this panel existed for the Everglades, it would carry: wetland extent
(historic ~4,000 sq mi, 10,360 km²; about half remains,
compartmentalised by levees and canals); the hydroperiod classes —
long-hydroperiod sloughs flooded 11+ months a year, intermediate-hydroperiod
sawgrass ridges 9–10 months, short-hydroperiod mixed-herb marshes 3–7
months (Davis & Ogden 1994); the water-level regime — a slope of less
than two inches per mile (≈3 cm/km, Florida Museum; the Davis & Ogden
overview gives ~2.5) from Lake Okeechobee, sheet flow crossing ~100 miles
(161 km) to Florida Bay, the flow now managed by structures rather than
by the lake's overflow; the phosphorus criterion — a long-term geometric
mean of 10 µg/L (10 ppb) total phosphorus, with flora and fauna beginning
to change just above it (Everglades Forever Act 1994; FDEP; Sklar et al.
2005); the vegetation flip — sawgrass dominant historically, cattail a
minor species before enrichment, cattail expanding from the 1970s in the
nutrient-enriched zones of Water Conservation Area 3A (198,727 ha) and
replacing sawgrass marsh (Brix et al. 2010; Belanger et al. 2009; Entry &
Gottlieb 2014); and the birds — 138,834 wading-bird nests across South
Florida in the 2018 season (122,571 in the Everglades), white ibis
100,784 nests, the strongest season since the 1930s; then white ibis
31,848 nests in 2022 and 22,003 in 2023 (−5% and −38% against the
10-year average); then the 2025 drought season, about 3,500 nests, the
lowest in 30 years against a 10-year average above 47,000 (SFWMD South
Florida Wading Bird Reports); and the long view — nesting populations of
five wading-bird species fell by as much as 90% from the 1930s to the
1980s (Ogden 1994).

The lesson the twin carries: the Everglades is the proof that a marsh is
its hydroperiod first and its chemistry second — the same marsh that
hosted 138,834 wading-bird nests in the 2018 season counted about 3,500
nests in the 2025 drought season, because the water did not arrive. The
phosphorus criterion (10 µg/L) is the second lesson: in an oligotrophic
marsh a few micrograms change the plant community — sawgrass gives way to
cattail — and the birds follow the plants. A twin that records only
"wetland area" misses both: the area can look stable while the hydroperiod
and the phosphorus quietly rewrite the marsh.

**Regional case: Mongol Daguur, Mongolia.** Mongol Daguur is a Ramsar
wetland (210,000 ha) and strictly protected area in Dornod Province,
north-eastern Mongolia, in the Ulz river basin — moist Daurian steppe with
lakes and ponds of different sizes, rivers, streams and reed-bed wetlands
(EAAF Site Information Sheet EAAF024, 2017; Ramsar Information Sheet
1997). It is the Mongolian side of the Dauria International Protected
Area and the regional case for this module. Had the panel existed for it,
it would carry: wetland extent (Ramsar site 210,000 ha, 2,100 km²; Mongol
Daguur SPA "A" 87,780 ha, SPA "B" 15,236 ha, buffer zone 484,425 ha —
UNESCO, Landscapes of Dauria nomination, 2017); waterbird use — staging
counts of 2,000 geese, 4,000 shelducks, 20,000 ducks, 7,000 coots and
over 10,000 gulls (Ramsar Information Sheet 1997), larger lakes supporting
tens of thousands of moulting waterbirds in summer, six crane species
including white-naped and Siberian cranes, and the Chuh Nuur area holding
over 10,000 waterfowl with at least eight globally threatened bird species
(UNESCO nomination 2017); vegetation — reed beds in the wetland areas,
lakes dominated by *Potamogeton* and other water plants in the wider
Daurian lake system; wetland-dependent fauna — swan goose, white-naped,
common, hooded, red-crowned and demoiselle cranes, great bustard, Asiatic
dowitcher, relict gull, falcated duck (Gombobaatar et al. 2011, via EAAF
SIS EAAF024). The neighbouring check is Ogii (Ugii) Lake, Arkhangai
Province — Ramsar site and Important Bird Area, 86 bird species recorded
in the avifaunal survey of Middle Mongolian wetlands, swan goose counts of
845–2,985 (Tsegmid & Uuganbayar 2006) and 2,706 counted in the survey
itself: one threatened goose species, counted in the hundreds to low
thousands, is the number the twin watches.
